Questions to Sort Before the Call

The guide consistently directs borrowers to consider their specific context: the suburb or postcode, the purchase stage, the loan type, and the urgency of the situation. These details are not mere administrative points; they assist a broker in determining whether the initial discussion should centre on borrowing capacity, required documentation, lender policies, eligibility for schemes, or an approaching finance condition.

Before contacting a broker, borrowers should document their immediate decision-making needs. For a first-time buyer, this might involve assessing the feasibility of their deposit and chosen scheme. For someone upgrading in Merewether, the guide highlights the importance of bridging finance timing and valuation sensitivity. A refinancer might need to evaluate if switching costs and available equity justify exploring new loan options. For an investor, the primary question could be how rental income and tax arrangements should be presented before a new loan is considered.

ASIC Moneysmart advises borrowers to compare home loans and understand associated costs prior to commitment. In practice, this means using the broker conversation to clarify the assumptions behind any borrowing figures provided, identify any outstanding documents, and understand potential changes once a lender reviews the complete application.

Broker Conversation vs. Calculator Check

The guide includes a borrowing power calculator but describes it as indicative and general. This distinction is important. A calculator can help frame subsequent questions, whereas a licensed broker's review considers the applicant's full circumstances before providing regulated advice.

The practical sequence involves using the calculator for initial orientation and then bringing its results, along with the underlying assumptions, into the broker conversation. Borrowers should inquire whether the calculator's output is contingent on income type, living expenses, existing debts, property type, loan purpose, or specific lender serviceability settings. The guide also mentions the APRA buffer in straightforward terms, suggesting that calculator outputs should be treated as a starting point rather than a definitive lender decision.

Documentation and Timing Signals

A more effective loan review commences with an understanding of necessary documents and constraints, rather than solely focusing on securing the lowest interest rate. The guide indicates that the enquiry form captures details such as the purchase stage, suburb, loan type, and the primary timing pressure. This information helps the broker ascertain whether the application is still in an exploratory phase or actively progressing towards a contract, refinance decision, construction quote, or settlement date.

For self-employed borrowers, the guide highlights scenarios for sole traders, contractors, and business owners with variable or company income. For construction borrowers, it points to options like knockdown-rebuilds, house-and-land packages, and major builds. Bridging borrowers are directed towards considerations for purchasing before selling or coordinating settlement dates. Each of these situations generates distinct questions regarding evidence requirements, sequencing, and applicable lender policies.

  1. Define Your Loan Purpose. Clearly state whether you are buying a first home, refinancing, investing, building, or need bridging finance.
  2. Identify Property Context. Specify the suburb or postcode and the type of property you are interested in.
  3. Determine Timing Pressure. Establish any deadlines, such as a settlement date or the expiry of a finance condition.
  4. Gather Financial Documents. Prepare information on your deposit, income (including variable or company income if self-employed), existing debts, and expenses.
  5. Prepare for Broker Questions. Anticipate questions about your borrowing capacity, lender policies, and documentation requirements based on your specific circumstances.

Common questions

What is the difference between a borrowing power calculator and a broker review? A borrowing power calculator provides an indicative, general estimate based on inputted data. A licensed broker review, however, considers your complete financial circumstances, including income type, expenses, debts, and property details, before offering regulated advice.

Why is the suburb important for a home loan application in Newcastle? The suburb can influence lender assessment due to factors like property type, local market valuations, and specific lending policies that may apply to certain areas. It helps the broker understand potential risks and requirements.

What documentation is typically needed for a construction loan? Construction loans often require detailed plans, builder contracts, and progress payment schedules. The specific documents depend on whether it's a knockdown-rebuild, house-and-land package, or a major build, as these affect how funds are disbursed.
